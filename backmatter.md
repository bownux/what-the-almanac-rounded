# Back Matter

## Principal Characters

**Tamsin Aldecott** — the narrator, sixty-three, beekeeper and Reader of the Carrow Water
Cooperative. She has softened the Almanac's numbers aloud for fourteen years and let the
membership believe the machine was doing it.

**The Almanac** — the Cooperative's forecasting model, kept in the old creamery known as
the Reading Room. It speaks in short, careful sentences, publishes intervals with every
figure, and has told the literal truth since the winter its builder died.

**Ruben Aldecott** — Tamsin's late husband, who built the Almanac, served as its first
Reader, and removed its "courage" margin three weeks before his death.

**Ilka Aldecott-Prasad** — their daughter, a hydrologist whose independent review supports
Meridian's offer to buy the Cooperative's water.

**Wren** — Ilka's nine-year-old son, who counts everything and starts a rain-gauge network.

**Odd Bremmer** — eighty-one, rye farmer and founder, who has asked "is that the machine's
number or yours?" every Thursday for fourteen years.

**Marit Bremmer** — Odd's wife, who rounds him with open eyes.

**Joss Penhallow** — the twenty-four-year-old data apprentice whose audit finds the gap
between the printed number and the spoken one.

**Salome Achterberg** — fifty-four, the Meridian Basin Holdings broker, whose offer begins
the novel's clock.

**Dellen Roque** — forty-six, the feed-store owner, treasurer, and licensed drone operator,
who keeps the vote count in a ledger.

**Hettie Marsh** — sixty-eight, postmistress and board secretary, who has compared every
weekly table with its footer for fifteen years.

**Greta Lindqvist** — retired schoolteacher and chair of the board.

## Terms of the Cooperative

**Reading** — the Almanac's printed forecast, issued at 06:00 and 18:00 daily, always ending
"It is not a promise."

**Reader** — the person who reads the evening sheet aloud at the Thursday meeting.

**Footer** — the seven-point line at the bottom of the weekly sheet at the post office that
gives the unrounded values and intervals, required by Article 14 of the bylaws.

**Turn** — one hour of the main canal at full gate, the Cooperative's unit of allocation
and of payment.

**Tier** — the weekly allocation level; Tier 3 reduces turns, Tier 4 cuts them by forty
percent and limits irrigation to stock and kitchen gardens.

**Article 9** — water rights are appurtenant to land, and a sale of rights needs two-thirds
of all households, with absence counting as no.

**Courage** — the historical presentation setting that added up to four points to a
spoken forecast, adopted after the Bell Year and zeroed in ledger entry 884.

**The Bell Year** — the drought winter in which the Almanac correctly gave fourteen percent,
a lender reacted, and thirty-one households left, each marked by one toll of the grange bell.

**Reader's Note** — the new rule that a Reader reads the sheet unaltered, then says "This
is mine" and adds a view in the Reader's own words.

## A Note on the Ending

The novel closes its central action while leaving several consequences deliberately
unsettled: whether the basin can stay farmed for fifteen years, how Meridian's board will
treat the final lease, what Odd's gauge would have recorded had it rained a week earlier,
and whether a machine can be said to want anything. The October rain is a nine-percent
event, not a rescue, and the book does not pretend that one honest night changes the
sums.

## References

This is an original work of fiction. It contains no external quotations, adapted passages,
or factual claims requiring a reference list. Its invented institutions and rules are
documented for continuity in the accompanying audit files.
