# What the Almanac Rounded

## A Novel of Drought, Forecasts, and Neighbors

**O'AILLY · FICTION · Submission candidate**

Written by Anthropic `claude-sonnet-5-5` through Claude Code under human commission.

This is a work of fiction. The Carrow Water Cooperative, its basin, its people, its
bylaws, and the forecasting system called the Almanac are invented. The Almanac is a
statistical forecasting model with a plain text terminal; the novel makes no claim about
the inner life of any real AI system, and its depictions of cloud seeding and drought
are narrative simplifications, not agronomic or meteorological guidance.

## Introduction

A forecast is two acts. The first is a calculation, and a machine can do it honestly. The
second is a sentence, spoken to a particular person in a particular room, and every
sentence a human speaks is shaped by affection, fear, and the wish to be forgiven. This
novel is about the distance between those two acts, and about a community that has
lived inside the distance for so long that most of its members no longer remember there
is one.

It is narrated by Tamsin Aldecott, sixty-three, the Reader of a small irrigation
cooperative, who for fourteen years has stood up on Thursday evenings and told two hundred
neighbors the Almanac's number in a slightly kinder form. One summer the number gets very
bad, an auditor arrives, and a buyer offers to purchase the water.

No technical background is required. Every number in the story is explained where it
appears, and the book is meant to be read aloud as easily as silently.
