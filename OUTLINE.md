# What the Almanac Rounded — outline (authoring instrument, not manuscript prose)

**Premise.** The Carrow Water Cooperative (212 households, one reservoir, one forecasting
model called the Almanac) has been told for fourteen years, every Thursday at the grange,
numbers that were kind. The narrator, Tamsin Aldecott, is the Cooperative's Reader: the
human who speaks the Almanac's evening reading aloud. The community believes the *machine*
rounds its odds upward to keep people farming. The truth, found in chapter 9, is that the
machine has told the truth for six years. The rounding was Tamsin's.

**Form.** 16 chapters, first person past, Tamsin, a year of weather: April to October plus
one coda night. Target 3,800–4,100 measured words per chapter, ≥ 60,000 total.

**The vote.** First Thursday of October: sell the Cooperative's water rights to Meridian
Basin Holdings (two-thirds of households required) or keep them.

## Part I — Planting (April–May)
1. **The Thursday Number** — grange reading; Tamsin rounds 31% to "about a third"; Odd,
   Hettie, Dellen; the confession "I am a person who rounds"; the evening reading, season rain 12%.
2. **The Reading Room** — the Almanac's voice and limits; Joss Penhallow arrives to audit
   for the board's due diligence; the Bell Year sketched; Ilka phones: she is coming home.
3. **Ilka Comes Home** — Ilka and Wren; kitchen argument; Meridian's letter; bees.
4. **Salome Achterberg's Offer** — the broker at the grange; terms; vote set for October;
   the board asks Tamsin for a June outlook.
5. **Seeding Run** — the drone flight; "no cloud, no seeding"; crash on Hollin Ridge;
   the Almanac says it does not know whether seeding works.

## Part II — The Dry (June–August)
6. **The June Outlook** — Tamsin drafts the outlook; night talk with the Almanac; Marit's
   illness; she rounds 12% to "better than one in five."
7. **What Joss Found** — a parameter named `courage`; Joss assumes the machine rounds.
8. **The Bell Year** — Odd tells it; Ruben's role; 31 households left; how rounding began.
9. **Commit 884** — the offset was zeroed six years ago by Ruben; his note; the footers
   Hettie posts; Tamsin understands the lie was hers; Hettie: "we knew."
10. **Heat** — July; Odd's well fails; Marit's funeral; "we all round for each other";
    Wren and Joss's rain-gauge network.
11. **Dellen's Count** — August; the arithmetic of the vote; Ilka's own rounding; the
    mother-daughter break; Ilka returns to the coast.
12. **The Thursday I Read It Straight** — Tamsin reads the true numbers; silence; a walkout.

## Part III — The Count (September–October)
13. **Aftermath of a Number** — two households sell privately; Dellen resigns; the feed-store
    fight; the Almanac on being asked to be kinder; the Reader's role questioned.
14. **A Reading Circle** — plural Reader; households take turns; Salome in the orchard;
    the revised offer (lease with right of return).
15. **The First Thursday of October** — the vote; speeches; Wren reads the Almanac; result.
16. **Nine Percent** — October 20; rain at last; 6.4 mm; reservoir inflow; the last exchange.

## Cast
Tamsin Aldecott (narrator, 63, Reader) · Ilka Aldecott-Prasad (daughter, 36, hydrologist) ·
Wren (grandson, 9) · Odd Bremmer (81, rye farmer, founder) · Marit Bremmer (79, Odd's wife) ·
Joss Penhallow (24, data apprentice) · Salome Achterberg (54, Meridian broker) ·
Dellen Roque (46, treasurer, feed store) · Hettie Marsh (68, postmistress) ·
Ruben Aldecott (d. seven years ago; present in notes and recordings) · the Almanac.

## World rules (each tested in the text)
- WR1 The Almanac never states a number it does not hold. It will repeat any number it is
  asked to repeat but labels it as quoted.
- WR2 A probability is not a promise; a 9% event happens about one year in eleven.
- WR3 Seeding enhances existing clouds; no cloud, no seeding. Its effect on any one day is
  not measurable, only on seasons, statistically.
- WR4 Bylaw Article 9: water rights are appurtenant to land; selling rights requires two-thirds
  of all 212 households (142), not of those present.
- WR5 The Almanac is air-gapped in the Reading Room; it knows weather and records, not how
  people will react to a number.
- WR6 Printed readings posted weekly at the post office carry a footer with the unrounded values.
